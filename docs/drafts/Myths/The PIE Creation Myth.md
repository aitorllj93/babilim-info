---
title: The Proto-Indo-European Creation Myth
tags: 
  - culture/proto-indo-european
---
In the beginning, there was nothing. There was no light, there was no dark. It was a place devoid of heat's embrace and cold's bitter touch. It was a time before gods and before mortals. Within this nothingness, the seeds of the cosmos began to stir. From the boundless emptiness emerged two distinct mists, one warm, the other cold. These mists intertwined in a celestial dance marking the beginning of a primordial creation. From this cosmic confluence the first entities emerged, not gods or humans, but primordial beings, the architects of existence., Our story unfolds with three such beings being born from the cosmic womb. Among them were [[*Manu (PIE)|Manus]] and [[*Yemo|Yemo]], entities bearing semblance to man, brothers twinned by fate itself, their names meaning, first man and twin, the first, a priestly figure, the latter, a kingly figure. Their companion,, the third being, was a primordial cow whose existence symbolised sustenance and life, essential for the journey ahead., As the aeons passed and brothers traversed newborn cosmos, their thoughts birthed gods and goddesses, entities striving to impose order upon the chaos that bubbled from creation's depths. Yet the cosmos remained untamed, a realm of endless potential and peril. The cosmos needed order. Feeling the struggle between order and chaos, the weight of their solitude and the vast emptiness around them, Manus and Yemo yearned for stability, a sanctuary where gods could dwell and order could flourish. In a sacrifice born from love, necessity, and the need for order, Yemo offered himself to lay the foundation for the cosmos' sanctuary. Manus, with a heavy and grateful heart, accepted his brother's sacrifice, performing a ritual of significance. This had to be carried out properly so as not to upset the cosmos or the gods. And once the mortal blow had been carved, Yemo's body was separated into three pieces, his head, his torso, and his legs. From the sacrifice of Yemo's body, a world was crafted. His flesh became the fertile earth, his bones the mountains and stones, his blood the rivers and seas, and from his hair the verdant grass was laid upon the earth. Manus placed Yemo's eyes in the heavens, creating the sun above and the moon below, his brain transformed into clouds, and his final breath became the wind. Yet a world without life was a world unfulfilled, and so Manus then sacrificed their companion, the primordial cow, birthing the flora and fauna that would thrive upon the earth. And from the remnants of Yemo, Manus fashioned humanity, instilling in them qualities reflective of their divine origins, kingship, priesthood, warriors, and the essence of the common folk. Manus combined a piece of Yemo's head, torso, and legs to make a man in the image of Yemo, and said to him, that he would be king, for this would ensure the best chance of prosperity. And then from the final piece of Yemo's head, Manus made priests. From the final pieces, of his body he made warriors, and from the last pieces of Yemo's legs, Manus made the commoners and the farmers., Upon this newly created world, Manus sensed Yemo's presence, his living essence had become the lord of the other world, a realm for all those who die. Manus introduced the sacred rites of sacrifice, teaching that through these rituals, order and balance could be maintained, ensuring the cosmos' continual renewal. And so it was that the people lived, farming the land, and the cows became a symbol of prosperity. As they were such useful animals, humans believed that they could have only come as gifts from the gods. They used their hide for clothing, the bones for tools, urine for disinfectant, manure for fertilizer, the flesh for food, they could even use it’s milk to feed themselves and their children, and so the warriors were used to protect the people, and these sacred cows, the gifts of the gods. As the priest sacrificed the people and the cows to the cosmos, the gods tried to keep chaos at bay. The [[*Dyḗus|sky father]], the [[*Dʰéǵʰōm|earth mother]], the [[*Perkʷunos|storm god]], the [[*H₂epom Nepōts|ocean god]], fertility gods, and others kept the cosmos in order as time, passed. Yet, order and balance was a fragile state in this world, for chaos, ever cunning, found pathways to seep back in. Over time, as people stopped performing rituals or left the settlements, chaos manifested itself, eventually personifying into [[*Ngʷhi]], a three-headed, six-eyed serpent, who was the embodiment of chaos. This sly and vile creature, who, in an unprovoked attack, stole the sacred cows off the people's land, drove chaos into the heart of order that Manus and Yemo had strived to establish. The people called on the strongest of the warriors to retrieve the cattle, this warrior was named [[*Trito|Trito]], a name meaning third. He stepped forward, and accepted the challenge, and he spent the day preparing special libations and offering to drink this drink in honor of the storm god, a god of strength and power, the warrior's god., This drink allowed Trito to possess some of the strength of the storm god, enough to make him as, strong as *Ngʷhi, and with his strength he embarked on the quest to the border of his people's land, a place where mountains touched the sky. His journey led him high into the mountains, where he confronted Inri in a cave. There they fought, and it ended with the serpent's defeat, and with this, Trito could restore peace and order. He drove the rescued cattle out the cave, and headed back towards his land. When he arrived back at the settlement, he was declared a hero, he told the story of how he killed the three-headed six-eyed serpent, and from this, a poem was written about him, and this would ensure that his fame would never die. After this the priests offered the cow, the finest of the reclaimed herd, as a sacrifice to the gods. It’s smoke carried the people's prayers to the heavens, and this pleased the gods, and it sealed Chaos' defeat. Yet, the saga of creation is an eternal, cycle, where peace is but a precursor to the resurgence of Chaos, a reminder of the delicate balance of the cosmic order of things.
