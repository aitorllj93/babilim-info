## The Last Song of Numantia

In 133 BCE, as Roman siege engines surrounded the hilltop city of Numantia, the Celtiberian defenders chose death over slavery, consigning themselves and their families to flames rather than submit to foreign rule. This act of collective *devotio* was not mere desperation but the ultimate expression of a mythology that valued freedom as sacred, death as transformation, and the warrior's honor as cosmic necessity. The Celtiberians – those Celtic-speaking peoples who had merged with Iberian populations in east-central Hispania – created a unique mythological synthesis where Indo-European sky gods learned to dwell in Iberian mountain sanctuaries, and indigenous earth goddesses spoke through Celtic oracles.

## **Cosmology**

### *The Forge of Worlds*

Celtiberian cosmogony began not with primordial waters but with the cosmic smithy. In the beginning, the divine smith [[Sukellus]] hammered out the world on his mountain anvil, each strike sending sparks that became stars. The Duero and Tagus rivers flowed from the cooling water used to temper the earth-blade. This metallurgical creation myth reflected a culture where ironworking held sacred status and smiths were semi-divine figures.

The first beings emerged from the seven caves of the primordial mountain: warriors from iron ore, priests from silver veins, farmers from copper deposits. [[Ataecina]], the underworld goddess, taught them the arts of civilization, while [[Lugh|Lugus]] descended from the sky to bring light and skill. The cosmic order (*rego*) was maintained through perpetual conflict between the forces of cultivation and wilderness, settlement and mountain, sword and plow.

### *The Three Circles*

Celtiberian cosmography envisioned reality as three overlapping circles held together by a cosmic tree whose roots grew from the sacred mountain:

**Albos** (The White World): The celestial realm where solar deities like [[Lugh|Lugus]] and [[Neto|Neton]] dwelt in halls of bronze. Eagles and vultures served as messengers between this realm and earth. The Milky Way was the "War Trail" where dead heroes eternally pursued cosmic enemies.

**Bitus** (The Living World): The middle realm of humans, anchored by sacred mountains (*Idubeda*, *Moncayo*) that served as cosmic axes. Every hillfort (*castro*) was a microcosm with its own sacred center. Rivers were serpent-deities, forests were neutral zones between order and chaos.

**Dubno** (The Deep): The underworld accessed through caves, springs, and special shafts. Ruled by [[Ataecina]] and [[Endovelicus|Endovellicus]], it was not solely a realm of death but of transformation, healing, and hidden wisdom. The divine boar [[Endovelicus|Endovellicus]] could travel between all realms.

### *The Wolf-Time and Rebirth*

Celtiberian eschatology predicted the "Wolf-Time" (*Ulco-Tempos*) when cosmic order would fail: mountains would march against cities, rivers would flow backward, and the dead would walk. The war-god [[Neto|Neton]] would lead a final battle against the chaos wolves at the "Field of Truth." 

Yet destruction was not final. The cosmic smith would forge a new world from the remains of the old, and the culture heroes would return from their star-halls. This cyclical vision provided theological support for their resistance ideology – defeat was temporary, freedom eternal. The promise of cosmic renewal made individual sacrifice meaningful within the larger pattern.

### *The Lunar Reckoning*

Time was reckoned by a complex lunar calendar preserved partially in the Botorrita bronzes. The year was divided into a bright half (summer) ruled by [[Lugh|Lugus]] and a dark half (winter) ruled by [[Ataecina]]. Key temporal markers included:

- **Lugunassatis**: The harvest assembly when divine marriages were reenacted
- **Trinoxtion Samoni**: The "Three Nights of Summer's End" when boundaries between worlds dissolved
- **Ambiuolcios**: The circuit of purification when warriors renewed their vows
- **Contuciassatis**: The spring "assembly of restitution" for legal matters

Sacred time was fractal – mythic events simultaneously occurred in primordial time and were renewed through ritual repetition.

## **Deities**

The Celtiberian pantheon reflected cultural synthesis, with Celtic divine names attached to figures embodying both Indo-European and pre-Indo-European characteristics:

### Major Divinities

**[[Lugh|Lugus]]** (Lugus/Lugu)  
*Domain*: Sovereignty, arts, commerce, oaths  
*Symbols*: Triple visage, ravens, spear  
*Sacred Sites*: Uxama (Osma), possibly Lucus Augusti  
*Role*: Divine exemplar of multiple skills, oath-witness

**[[Endovelicus|Endovellicus]]** (Ende-vell-ikos)  
*Domain*: Prophecy, healing, underworld passages  
*Symbols*: Boar, pine cone, crescent moon  
*Sacred Sites*: Cave sanctuaries throughout Lusitania  
*Role*: Oracular deity consulted through incubation

**[[Ataecina|Ataegina]]** (Ataicina/Adaegina)  
*Domain*: Spring goddess, rebirth, underworld justice  
*Symbols*: Goat, cypress branch, keys  
*Sacred Sites*: Turibriga, Mérida region  
*Role*: Enforcer of curses, protector of transitions

**[[Neto|Neton]]** (Neto/Neito)  
*Domain*: War, solar aspects, cosmic order  
*Symbols*: Solar wheel, wolf, spear and shield  
*Sacred Sites*: Accis (Cádiz), warrior sanctuaries  
*Role*: Divine warrior, maintainer through conflict

**[[Epona]]** (Epona/Ipouna)  
*Domain*: Horses, sovereignty, psychopomp  
*Symbols*: Mare with foal, cornucopia, keys  
*Sacred Sites*: Scattered shrines near horse pastures  
*Role*: Protector of cavalry, guide to otherworld

**[[Bandua]]** (Bandue/Bandi)  
*Domain*: Protective deity of communities  
*Symbols*: Fortress walls, standard, bound figures  
*Sacred Sites*: Multiple local variants (Bandua Aetobrigus, etc.)  
*Role*: Guardian of social cohesion and boundaries

**[[Trebaruna|Trebarune]]** (Trebaronne/Trebaruna)  
*Domain*: Victory in battle, domestic protection  
*Symbols*: House altar, victory palm  
*Sacred Sites*: Northwestern territories  
*Role*: Household guardian scaling to war camps

### Regional Variations

Many deities appeared in highly localized forms:
- **Crouga** (Gallaecia): Boundary stone deity
- **Nabia** (Northwest): River and fountain goddess  
- **Reue** (Multiple regions): Mountain deity associated with passes
- **Arentius/Arentia** (Lusitania): Paired deities of hot springs
- **Quangeius** (Central regions): Protector of cattle

## **Pantheon**

### *Divine Genealogies*

The Celtiberian pantheon lacked the elaborate genealogies of Mediterranean mythologies, instead organizing deities by functional pairs and triads:

**The War Triad**:
- [[Neto|Neton]] (Solar warrior)
- [[Bandua]] (Fortress guardian)  
- [[Trebaruna|Trebarune]] (Victory bringer)

**The Prophecy Dyad**:
- [[Endovelicus|Endovellicus]] (Oracular healing)
- [[Ataecina|Ataegina]] (Chthonic wisdom)

**The Sovereignty Complex**:
- [[Lugh|Lugus]] (Multiple arts)
- [[Epona|Epona]] (Horse goddess)
- Local protective deities (*deis totatibus*)

### *Gods of Place*

Celtiberian religion was intensely localized, with most communities worshipping:
- A protective deity specific to their settlement
- Regional forms of pan-Celtic gods  
- Deified landscape features
- Ancestral collective spirits (*deis parentibus*)

This theological localism created a religious landscape where every mountain pass, spring, and grove potentially housed divinity, mapped through an oral tradition now lost.

### *Divine Syncretism*

The merger of Celtic and Iberian traditions produced unique theological concepts:
- Celtic sky gods inhabiting Iberian sacred mountains
- Iberian chthonic deities speaking through Celtic ritual forms
- Shared emphasis on warrior ideologies enhancing both traditions
- Technology deities (especially metallurgy) bridging cultural gaps

## **Myths, Tales, and Stories**

### *The Theft of Sacred Fire*

When humans dwelt in darkness, the culture hero Olyndicus climbed the cosmic mountain to steal fire from [[Sukellus|Sukellus's]] forge. The divine smith pursued him in the form of a giant eagle, but Olyndicus hid the flames in a hollow fennel stalk. As punishment and reward, [[Sukellus]] taught him ironworking but bound him to forge weapons for both gods and humans, maintaining the cosmic balance through eternal labor.

### *The Lusitanian Shepherd*

Viriathus began as a shepherd who discovered a golden sickle in a wolf's den. With it, he could cut through any material and understand animal speech. The wolves revealed they were cursed warriors who had betrayed their *devotio*. By using the sickle to cut their bonds, Viriathus freed them to die properly, and they granted him their ferocity in battle. His eight-year resistance against Rome proved the gods still favored those who kept ancient oaths. Upon his assassination, [[Lugh|Lugus]] transformed him into a star to lead future liberators.

### *The Speaking Head of Belo*

King Belo possessed a bronze head that prophesied truly. When enemies threatened, the head would speak strategies that never failed. But Belo grew proud and ignored the head's warning against breaking hospitality laws. When he murdered a guest for treasure, the head cursed him: "Your own head shall speak truth to your enemies." Captured and beheaded by rivals, Belo's severed head revealed his treasury's location, teaching that divine gifts are withdrawn from oath-breakers.

### *The Mare Who Was a Queen*

[[Epona]] appeared to a childless queen as a white mare, promising progeny if she would nurse the mare's foal. The queen agreed, and her milk transformed the foal into a warrior who conquered all enemies. But when the queen boasted she had created him alone, [[Epona]] reclaimed her son. The queen realized her error and established the horse-feeding rituals still practiced at sovereignty ceremonies.

### *The Smith's Three Masterworks*

[[Sukellus]] promised to forge three supreme works for the hero who brought him water from the underworld spring, fire from the sun's chariot, and wind from the storm god's bag. The hero Ambatus succeeded through cunning and courage. [[Sukellus]] forged: a sword that could cut fate itself, a shield that reflected truth, and a torc that compelled honest speech. With these, Ambatus established justice throughout the land, though the relics vanished upon his death, awaiting a worthy successor.

### *The Hospitality of Segobrix*

Chief Segobrix sheltered a travelling bard who was secretly [[Lugh|Lugus]] in disguise. Despite famine, he shared his last bread and defended the stranger from his own warriors who counseled expelling him. Impressed, [[Lugh|Lugus]] revealed himself and granted Segobrix's people eternal plenty, but warned: "When you cease welcoming strangers, this blessing ends." The tale reinforced hospitality laws as cosmic necessity.

### *The War Against the Moon*

When the moon goddess stole the sun's light, plunging earth into darkness, [[Neto|Neton]] assembled a war band of dead heroes to reclaim it. They built a tower of shields to reach the moon, but each night it collapsed. Finally, a nameless warrior suggested using hospitality rather than force. They invited the moon to a feast, and through gifts and praise, negotiated the return of half the light, establishing the lunar cycle. The myth explained both celestial phenomena and the superiority of diplomacy over force.

### *The Nine Hazels of Wisdom*

Nine hazel trees grew around [[Endovelicus|Endovellicus's]] oracular pool, dropping nuts that granted prophecy to the sacred boars who ate them. A young warrior sought wisdom by eating the hazelnuts himself but saw only his own death. [[Ataecina]] took pity and explained: "Wisdom isn't knowing the future but accepting fate while living fully." She transformed him into the first priest-interpreter of oracles, teaching that divine knowledge required divine mediation.

## **Rituals**

### *The Devotio Iberica*

The most extreme expression of Celtiberian religiosity was the *devotio*, where warriors pledged their lives to their chieftain, following him even into death. This practice combined Celtic warrior-band traditions with Iberian loyalty customs, creating a institution both military and mystical. The devoted ones (*soldurii*) underwent ritual death and rebirth, thereafter existing in a liminal state between worlds. If their chief died naturally, they committed ritual suicide to maintain the cosmic bargain.

### *Mountain Sanctuaries*

Sacred peaks like Peñalba de Villastar served as cosmic centers where rock-carved inscriptions invoked deities in Celtiberian language. Rituals included:
- Dawn prayers to catch first light
- Weapon deposits in summit cairns
- Vision quests through exposure and fasting
- Thunder ceremonies during storms
- Astronomical observations for calendar keeping

### *The Horse Sacrifice*

The October Horse ritual (*Equus October*) was adapted to honor [[Epona]] and confirm sovereignty. The fastest horse was raced, then sacrificed. Its blood was mixed with last year's ashes and sprinkled on fields and fortifications. The head was fought over by community factions, with the winners gaining divine favor for the coming year. The tail was fastened to the chief's house as a protective talisman.

### *Water Oracles*

At healing springs sacred to [[Endovelicus|Endovellicus]], suppliants underwent ritual purification before sleeping in the sanctuary. Dreams were interpreted by specialized priests who prescribed cures combining practical medicine with ritual acts. Votive offerings recovered from springs include miniature body parts, tablets inscribed with thanks, and deliberately bent weapons "killed" to accompany healing.

### *The Tessera Hospitalis*

Hospitality agreements were sealed by breaking a bronze token (tessera) shaped like clasped hands or animals. Each party kept half, creating hereditary bonds transcending death. The ritual included:
1. Sacrificing to [[Lugh|Lugus]] as oath witness
2. Sharing bread and salt
3. Exchanging genealogies
4. Breaking the tessera over an altar
5. Invoking curses on oath-breakers

Violating these agreements brought supernatural punishment from both gods and ancestors.

### *Cremation Mysteries*

The predominant cremation ritual followed elaborate protocols:
1. Warriors burned with their weapons "killed" by bending
2. Women with jewelry and weaving implements
3. Chiefs with their devoted followers
4. Sacred meals consumed beside the pyre
5. Ashes placed in painted urns with geometric designs
6. Goods "killed" to serve in afterlife
7. Feasting continued until fire died

The orientation of the pyre, timing (preferably at dusk), and specific woods used all carried symbolic meaning lost to us but clearly codified in their practice.

### *Seasonal Festivals*

**Beltane Equivalent**: Though the Celtic name is unattested, spring festivals involved:
- Cattle driven between twin fires for purification  
- Young warriors' initiation rites
- Sacred marriages between chiefs and sovereignty goddesses
- Competitive games establishing hierarchy

**Lughnasa Adaptations**: Harvest festivals honoring [[Lugh|Lugus]] featured:
- First fruits offered at mountain shrines
- Trade fairs doubling as religious gatherings
- Judicial assemblies under divine witness
- Storytelling competitions preserving mythology

**Samhain Parallels**: The autumn transition when:
- Ancestors walked among the living
- Warriors renewed their *devotio* oaths
- Divination for the coming year
- Strategic alliances negotiated under supernatural sanction

### *War Rituals*

Before battle, elaborate ceremonies invoked divine favor:
1. Reading omens from bird flight (*ornithomancy*)
2. Sacrificing captives to [[Neto|Neton]]
3. Drinking from skull cups of defeated enemies
4. Dancing naked except for weapons and torcs
5. Individual combat between champions
6. Vowing temple treasures if victorious
7. Self-dedication rituals ensuring glorious death

Post-battle rituals included displaying heads at temple doors and depositing spoils in sacred lakes.

## **Associated Cultures**

### *Predecessors*

**Bronze Age Iberians**: The Celtiberians inherited from earlier populations:
- Sacred mountain cult sites
- Metallurgical religious practices
- Bull imagery and rituals
- Geometric artistic traditions
- Cave sanctuary networks

**Urnfield Culture**: Celtic elements arrived with:
- Cremation practices
- Warrior ideology  
- Solar symbolism
- Hilltop settlement patterns
- Proto-Celtic language

**Atlantic Bronze Age**: Maritime connections brought:
- Warrior stelae traditions
- Prestige goods networks
- Shared mythological mythemes
- Bronze deposition rituals

### *Contemporaries*

**Lusitanians**: Western neighbors sharing:
- [[Endovelicus|Endovellicus]] worship
- Warrior band structures
- Mountain sanctuary practices
- Resistance ideology against Rome

**Vaccaei**: Northern contacts contributing:
- Collectivist social structures
- Grain storage technologies with ritual aspects
- Shared festivals and markets
- Military alliances with religious oaths

**Iberians**: Eastern interactions producing:
- Written script adaptations
- Artistic style fusion
- Urban planning concepts
- Trade route sanctification

**Gauls**: Distant Celtic cousins maintaining:
- Druidic knowledge exchange
- Shared deity names
- Similar social structures
- Common ritual practices

### *Descendants and Legacy*

**Roman Period Syncretism**: Under Rome, Celtiberian deities merged with:
- [[Mars]] absorbed [[Neto|Neton's]] attributes
- [[Diana]] syncretized with [[Ataecina]]
- [[Mercury]] merged with [[Lugh|Lugus]]
- Local cults persisted as *genius loci*

**Visigothic Survival**: Elements enduring into Christian period:
- Mountain pilgrimages to former sanctuaries
- Water cult sites became holy wells
- Warrior saints replaced war gods
- Festival dates preserved in Christian calendar

**Medieval Folklore**: Mythological survivals in:
- *Cantar de Mio Cid* echoing devotio themes
- Mountain spirit traditions
- Metamorphosis tales from deity myths
- Treasure guardian legends at ancient sites

**Modern Traces**:
- Place names preserving theonyms
- Folk festivals with pre-Christian elements
- Archaeological tourism to sacred sites
- Neo-pagan revival movements
- Regional identity movements invoking Celtiberian heritage

## **Language and Etymology**

### *Linguistic Heritage*

Celtiberian represents the only extensively attested Continental Celtic language of the Iberian Peninsula, written in both indigenous semi-syllabic script and Latin alphabet:

**Theonyms**:
- *Lugus* < Proto-Celtic *\*lugus* "light, brightness"
- *Neton* < *\*neitos* "warrior, hero" + augmentative *-on*
- *Endovellicus* < *\*ande-* "very" + *\*vello-* "better" + Latin suffix
- *Ataegina* < Celtic *\*ate-* "re-" + *\*gena* "born"
- *Trebaruna* < *\*trebo-* "settlement" + *\*runa* "mystery, secret"

**Sacred Terminology**:
- *Nemeton* - "sacred grove"
- *Vereia* - "warrior band" (cf. Latin vir)
- *Tessera* - "hospitality token" with religious significance
- *Kar* - "stone, sacred stone" in topography

**Inscriptional Evidence**:

The Botorrita bronze tablets preserve religious language:
- *Trecaias to luguei* - "The third (portion) to Lugus"
- *Eniorosei equeisuique* - "To Eniorosis and to the horsemen" 
- Religious formulas mixing Celtic and Iberian elements

## **Art and Iconography**

### *Warrior Stelae*

The distinctive Celtiberian funerary monuments featured:
- Central warrior figure with round shield (caetra)
- Spears, swords, and horse imagery
- Solar discs and lunar crescents
- Vultures as psychopomps
- Geometric patterns encoding clan identities

### *Metalwork Mastery*

**Fibulae** (Brooches):
- Horse-shaped brooches invoking [[Epona]]
- Zoomorphic designs featuring boars, wolves, and bulls
- Geometric patterns with cosmological significance
- Precious metal indicating divine favor

**Weapons as Art**:
- Pattern-welded sword blades with serpentine designs
- Decorated scabbards showing ritual scenes
- Horse gear with protective symbols
- Shield bosses as solar emblems

### *Ceramic Traditions*

Painted pottery revealed mythological narratives:
- Geometric patterns representing cosmic order
- Animal processions with ritual significance
- Abstract human figures in dance or combat
- Narrative scenes possibly depicting myths

### *Sacred Architecture*

Though modest compared to Mediterranean temples:
- Circular structures for celestial observations
- Rock-cut altars oriented to astronomical events
- Sacred pools with stone surrounds
- Votive deposit pits carefully positioned

## **Comparative Mythology**

### *Indo-European Parallels*

Celtiberian mythology preserved archaic Indo-European features:
- Tripartite social ideology (priests, warriors, producers)
- Divine horse twins (cf. Vedic Ashvins, Greek Dioscuri)
- Storm god fighting chaos serpent
- Fire-water cosmogonic opposition
- Sacred hospitality obligations

### *Celtic Connections*

Shared features with insular Celtic traditions:
- Sovereignty goddess choosing kings
- Otherworld as ever-young paradise
- Shape-shifting deities
- Magical cauldrons of plenty
- Head cult and prophetic skulls
- Sacred numbers (especially 3 and 9)

### *Mediterranean Influences*

Contact with classical world introduced:
- Written votive dedications
- Anthropomorphic deity representations
- Temple architecture concepts
- Mystery cult elements
- Philosophical interpretations of mythology

### *Iberian Synthesis*

Indigenous elements creating unique features:
- Mountain-centered cosmology
- Bull/boar symbolic complex
- Metallurgical creation myths
- Specific warrior devotion practices
- Cave oracle traditions

## **Philosophical and Ethical Teachings**

### *The Warrior's Path*

Celtiberian ethics centered on the concept of *virtus* (borrowed into Latin from Celtic):
- **Fides**: Loyalty to sworn companions unto death
- **Hospitium**: Sacred guest-friendship extending beyond death
- **Devotio**: Spiritual dedication merging individual with collective
- **Libertas**: Freedom as the highest value, worth any sacrifice

The Numantine choice of collective suicide over slavery exemplified these ideals in practice.

### *Cosmic Balance*

The world required constant maintenance through ritual action:
- Warriors maintained cosmic order through honorable combat
- Smiths participated in divine creation through metalwork
- Priests balanced the three realms through proper sacrifice
- Women ensured continuity through sovereignty rituals

### *Death and Transcendence*

Death was transformation, not ending:
- Warriors who died in battle joined the eternal war-band
- Smiths entered the mountain forges of [[Sukellus]]
- Poets became voices in sacred groves
- Healers merged with spring spirits

The emphasis on cremation released the spirit for immediate transformation.

### *Social Philosophy*

Mythology reinforced social structures:
- **Warrior Bands**: Divine precedent in [[Neto|Neton's]] celestial host
- **Hospitality Laws**: Based on divine visitations in disguise
- **Collective Decision-Making**: Reflecting divine assemblies
- **Women's Authority**: Grounded in sovereignty goddess traditions

### *Resistance Theology*

The struggle against Rome developed theological dimensions:
- Foreign domination violated cosmic order
- Death in resistance guaranteed divine favor
- Betrayal brought supernatural punishment
- Liberty was divinely ordained for mountain peoples

Viriathus's elevation to stellar status provided the model for resistance martyrology.

### *Wisdom Literature*

Though oral tradition is lost, inscriptions suggest maxims:
- "The gods love courage more than victory"
- "Iron bends but honor breaks never"
- "Guest-friendship outlasts bronze"
- "Mountains witness oaths when men forget"

These fragments indicate a sophisticated ethical tradition comparable to other Indo-European wisdom literatures.

### *Nature and Divinity*

The Celtiberian vision saw nature as transparently divine:
- Every spring held healing power
- Mountains were literally divine bodies
- Storms manifested divine emotions
- Animals could be divine messengers or gods in disguise

This theological framework encouraged ecological awareness and ritual attention to natural phenomena, creating a mythology inseparable from its landscape.
