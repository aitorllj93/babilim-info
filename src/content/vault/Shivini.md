##  Introduction
Shivini (also known as Siuini, Artinis, or Ardinis) was one of the principal deities in the Urartian pantheon, serving as the solar god of the ancient Iron Age kingdom of Urartu that flourished in the Armenian Highlands from the 9th to 6th centuries BCE. As the third member of the supreme divine trinity alongside Khaldi and Theispas, Shivini embodied the power and life-giving properties of the sun. He was typically depicted as a man kneeling on one knee while holding up a solar disc, symbolizing his role as the bearer of celestial light.

---

## Etymology

- **Root meaning** of the name: The exact etymology remains somewhat unclear
- **Language of origin**: Urartian, with possible Hittite influences
- **Variants and evolution**: Also known as Siuini, Artinis, and Ardinis in Urartian texts
- **Symbolic or metaphoric implications**: According to scholar Armen Petrosyan, Shivini's name may derive from a Hittite source and share the same Indo-European origin as the names of Ancient Greek Zeus and Roman Jupiter

Shivini was also called Šimigi by the Hurrians, suggesting linguistic and cultural connections between these neighboring peoples.

> _"The name derives from a Hittite source, and is, therefore, of the same Indo-European origin as the names of Ancient Greek Zeus and Roman Jupiter."_  
> — Armen Petrosyan, Journal of the Society for Armenian Studies

##  Cultural Context

- **Civilization**: Kingdom of Urartu (also known as the Kingdom of Van)
- **Geographic Region**: Armenian Highlands, centered around Lake Van in present-day eastern Turkey
- **Historical Period**: Iron Age, 9th-6th centuries BCE
- **Alternative Names**:
  - Šiuini – Urartian variant
  - Šimigi – Hurrian equivalent
  - Artinis/Ardinis – Alternative Urartian names

## Attributes and Domains

- **Domains of power**: Sun, light, justice, celestial guidance
- **Symbols**: Winged solar disc, shield
- **Sacred animals**: Unknown specific sacred animals
- **Elemental associations**: Fire, light, celestial bodies

| Attribute       | Meaning                        |
|----------------|---------------------------------|
| Solar Disc | Symbol of divine power and life-giving light |
| Winged Shield | Protection and celestial movement |

Shivini's attribute was a winged shield, which was a widespread motif in the civilizations of Ancient Egypt, Mesopotamia, and the Hittite state. The shield served as a symbol of the solar deity and represented both protection and the sun's journey across the sky.

## Myths and Legends

Unfortunately, no complete mythological texts about Shivini have survived. What we know comes primarily from inscriptions, dedications, and artistic representations. 

Urartian kings invoked Shivini along with Khaldi and Theispas before military campaigns, seeking divine blessing for their endeavors. One such prayer was inscribed by King Argishti I on the Van Rock in Tushpua:

> _"On the third day, I gathered the warriors and raised prayers to Haldi, the ruler, to the god Theispas, to the god Shivini, to all the gods of the land of Biainila for what I wished to accomplish for the glory of the Lord; the gods heard my request."_

**Other Associations**  
- Shivini corresponded to the Assyrian sun god Shamash, who was also associated with justice and wisdom
- In Urartian cuneiform, his name was written using an Assyrian ideogram, highlighting the cultural exchange between these civilizations

---

## Worship and Rituals

- **Forms of worship**: According to King Menua's inscriptions, Shivini's worship required the sacrifice of 4 oxen and 8 sheep
- **Festivals**: Specific festivals are unknown but likely followed solar cycles
- **Sacred spaces**: The Urartian capital of Tushpa was probably the center of Shivini's cult
- **Archaeological remains**: Bronze cauldrons and figurines depicting Shivini have been discovered in Urartian sites

One particularly notable artifact is a sacred bronze cauldron guarded by winged avatars of Shivini, showcasing his importance in Urartian religious art and ritual practices.

---

## Later Influence

- **Syncretism**: Shivini shared characteristics with the Hurrian deity Šimigi and the Assyrian Shamash
- **Legacy in later religions**: Elements of solar worship continued in later Armenian religious traditions
- **Presence in modern culture**: Depicted on an Armenian postage stamp in 1993, showing continued cultural significance

## Modern Interpretations

- **Comparative mythology**: Shivini represents a common pattern of solar deities in ancient Near Eastern religions
- **Recent academic theories**: Recent archaeological discoveries at sites like Ayanis have provided new insights into Urartian religious practices and the role of Shivini in the pantheon
