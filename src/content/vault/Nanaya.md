## Introduction

Nanaya was an Akkadian and Babylonian goddess of sensuality, love, and sexual pleasure, revered throughout Mesopotamia from the third millennium BCE onwards. Often associated with [[Inanna]]/[[Ishtar]], she embodied the erotic and sensual aspects of femininity, serving as a divine patron of lovers and courtesans. Her worship spread beyond Mesopotamia to Syria, Elam, and even into the Hellenistic period, where she became syncretized with various local deities.

---

## Etymology

- **Root meaning**: The name "Nanaya" (Akkadian: 𒀭𒈾𒈾𒀀, *Na-na-a*) is of uncertain origin, possibly deriving from a Sumerian diminutive or affectionate form related to "Inanna."
- **Language of origin**: Akkadian, with possible Sumerian antecedents
- **Variants and evolution**: 
  - Sumerian: *Nanaya*
  - Akkadian: *Nanāya*, *Nanâ*
  - Later identified with *Tasmetu* (consort of [[Nabu]])
  - Hellenistic: *Nanaia*
- **Symbolic or metaphoric implications**: The name suggests intimacy and affection, reflecting her role as a goddess of personal, intimate love rather than cosmic fertility

> _"Nanaya, the lady of voluptuousness, whose charms are irresistible"_  
> — Babylonian hymn, circa 1800 BCE

---

## Cultural Context

- **Civilization**: Akkadian, Babylonian, Assyrian
- **Geographic Region**: Mesopotamia (modern-day Iraq), with cult centers in Uruk, Babylon, and Borsippa
- **Historical Period**: Attested from Early Dynastic III period (c. 2600 BCE) through the Seleucid period (c. 300 BCE)
- **Alternative Names**:
  - *Nanâ* – shortened form used in later periods
  - *Tasmetu* – syncretic identification as consort of [[Nabu]]
  - *Nanaia* – Hellenistic form

## Myths and Legends

**Nanaya and the Sacred Marriage**  
Nanaya played a role in the sacred marriage rituals (*hieros gamos*) performed in Mesopotamian temples, where she represented the divine bride whose union with the king ensured fertility and prosperity for the land. Though often conflated with [[Inanna]], Nanaya maintained her distinct identity as the goddess of intimate, personal love.

> _"Nanaya, adorned with precious stones, her body anointed with sweet-smelling oil, awaits her beloved in the bedchamber of the gods."_  
> — Temple hymn from Uruk, Old Babylonian period

**The Courtship of Lovers**  
In various love incantations and prayers, young lovers invoked Nanaya to grant them success in courtship, to make them desirable to their beloved, and to ensure passionate unions. She was the divine patron of human sexuality in its most pleasurable aspects.

**Nanaya's Journey to Borsippa**  
In later Babylonian tradition, Nanaya became closely associated with [[Nabu]], the god of wisdom and writing, as his consort Tasmetu. This union symbolized the marriage of erotic love with intellectual pursuits.

## Worship and Rituals

- **Forms of worship**: Offerings of perfume, cosmetics, jewelry; prayers for successful love affairs; rituals of adornment
- **Festivals**: Celebrations during the New Year (*Akitu*) festival; private rituals performed by lovers
- **Sacred spaces**: 
  - Temple *E-anna* in Uruk (shared with [[Inanna]])
  - Shrine in Babylon
  - Temple *E-zida* in Borsippa (as Tasmetu)
- **Archaeological remains**: 
  - Terracotta plaques depicting nude female figures associated with Nanaya
  - Cylinder seals showing worship scenes
  - Dedicatory inscriptions from various Mesopotamian cities
  - A temple dedicated to Nanaia in Dura-Europos (Hellenistic period)

---

## Later Influence

- **Syncretism**: 
  - Identified with [[Ishtar]] in her aspect of sexual love
  - Merged with Tasmetu, consort of [[Nabu]]
  - Syncretized with Anahita in Persian tradition
  - Associated with Artemis Nanaia in Hellenistic Syria
- **Legacy in later religions**: Her cult survived into the Parthian period; some scholars suggest influences on later Gnostic and Manichaean concepts of divine femininity
- **Presence in modern literature and culture**: Featured in academic studies of ancient sexuality and gender; referenced in modern reconstructionist pagan movements

## Modern Interpretations

- **Psychological/archetypal readings**: Represents the archetype of the sacred courtesan, embodying sexuality as a divine gift rather than a profane act; Jung might classify her as an aspect of the Anima in its erotic manifestation
- **Comparative mythology**: Similar to Aphrodite (Greek), Venus (Roman), Hathor (Egyptian) in erotic aspects, though more focused on personal rather than cosmic love
- **Recent academic theories or discoveries**: Recent scholarship emphasizes Nanaya's independent identity rather than viewing her merely as an aspect of [[Inanna]]; studies of gender and sexuality in ancient Mesopotamia have highlighted her unique role in personal devotion
